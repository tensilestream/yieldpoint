# Commercial licensing

Yieldpoint is free in production for individuals, for teams of fewer than ten people
who write or review software, and for charities, schools and open source projects. It is
free for everyone, without limit, for evaluation, development and any non-production use.

Beyond that, production use needs a commercial licence. The exact terms live in
[LICENSE](./LICENSE); this page is the plain-English version.

## Do I need to pay?

| You are | Production use |
|---|---|
| One person, your own work | **Free** |
| A team of fewer than 10 people writing or reviewing software | **Free** |
| A charity, university, or an OSI-licensed open source project | **Free** |
| Anyone evaluating, developing against, or testing it | **Free**, without limit |
| A company of 10 or more people writing or reviewing software | **Commercial licence** |

Headcount means people who write or review software in your organisation — not total
employees. A 400-person company with six engineers is free.

## What you get either way

The source, all of it. Every rule, every test, the whole engine. There is no crippled
build, no feature held back for paying customers today, and no telemetry: the package
makes no network call, which you can verify rather than take on trust.

## What you may not do on the free tier

Circumvent, disable, or remove licence key, usage limit or metering functionality. That
restriction is in the licence itself rather than left to the code, because in
source-available software anyone can delete a check — what makes removing it *actionable*
is that the licence forbids it.

## It becomes Apache-2.0

Every released version converts to the Apache License, Version 2.0 four years after its
release. That is written into the licence and cannot be withdrawn. If this project is
abandoned, or the terms change in a way you dislike, the version you have today still
becomes fully open source on schedule.

## Getting a licence

Open an issue, or write privately through
[GitHub Security Advisories](https://github.com/tensilestream/yieldpoint/security/advisories/new)
if you would rather not discuss procurement in public. Tell us roughly how many engineers
you have; that is all the pricing depends on.
